The model: ground and strata

The graph has two layers, following German lexicography (Augst 2009; Splett 2009). The surface holds present-day word families: words a speaker today feels belong together. The underground holds history: older stages of the root, back as far as reliable sources reach. Both layers carry their sources on every link.

Real data for fahren. Lines on the surface are derivation links (each carries its sources). The Proto-Indo-European root is stored but not shown, because only one source gives it. Principal parts from UniMorph: fährt, fuhr, gefahren.

Compounds are bridges, not family members. Fahrzeug links the families of fahren and Zeug; it does not merge them. The same holds for the old causative pairs (liegen/legen, sitzen/setzen): related in history, but no longer a living pattern (Fleischer & Barz 2012).

How words are linked

Each link records its kind (deriv, compound_head, compound_mod, causative) and every source that supports it. In the full lexicon there are 48,561 derivation links: 25,350 with one source, 23,211 with two or more.

Family rule
  • Links with 2+ independent sources may merge families freely (60 of 60 correct in a random sample).
  • A one-source link (57 of 60 correct) may attach a single word to a family but may never merge two families. One wrong link (Anfuhr → anführen) would otherwise fuse fahren and führen.
  • Particles (an, auf, aus, fest…) are affixes on verbs and never a family base. Without this rule one family grew to 21,878 words.

How history is attached

Stages

Each family gets the chain of its root word: the entry's own templates, then the Proto-West-Germanic and Proto-Germanic reconstruction pages. Middle and Old High German forms are confirmed against German Wiktionary where possible (1,427 of 4,093 stages carry two sources). Stages Wiktionary marks as uncertain carry an origin_uncertain flag.

Proto-Indo-European: two independent sources

Claims from English Wiktionary, German Wiktionary and IE-CoR (an expert cognate database) are compared by consonant skeleton. Vowel grades, laryngeals, i/y, u/w and the optional s- are normalised, so *yóh₁r̥ and *i̯eh₁-r/n- (Jahr) count as the same root.

Loans

Loan origins come from the word's entry or the reconstruction pages (Fenster ← Latin fenestra, Straße ← Late Latin strāta): 851 families. Origins in a proto-language are never asserted. They are either hypotheses (Leder ← Proto-Celtic?) or reversed (Burg: Slavic borrowed it from Germanic).

Levels

The official CEFR lists (Goethe-Institut) are copyrighted, so the game uses an estimated level built only from openly licensed evidence:

  • Deutsch im Blick chapter (first-year course, CC BY): chapters 1–5 → A1, 6–10 → A2.
  • MERLIN learner texts (CC BY-SA): the lowest rated level at which 2+ learners use the word.
  • Subtitle frequency per lemma (OpenSubtitles, CC BY-SA) on a 1k / 2k / 4k / 8k / 16k scale.
  • Family cap: a transparent compound or derivation is at most one level above its parts (Bauer & Nation 1993).

Rule: the course chapter if the word is in the course, else the lower of subtitle and learner level, then the family cap. It was chosen among 13 variants by agreement with the Goethe A1–B1 lists, which are used only for this comparison: 32% exact, 76% within one level. The lists favour exam topics (Abfahrt, Schrank, ankreuzen) over frequency, which sets a ceiling on any frequency-based estimate.

Checks on every build

validate.py stops a build when any check fails. On its first run it caught three wrong loan claims (Burg, Leder, Zaun) that are now fixed.

Known gaps

  • Precision was measured by one reviewer on 160 links; a second reviewer would make the figures firmer.
  • 1,506 of 3,547 families have no history yet, mostly modern compounds and formations whose history lives in their parts.
  • Single-source compounds from German Wiktionary sometimes take a definition for an origin (Samt ← Seidengewebe).
  • Transparency per link (how obvious stehen → verstehen is) is not scored yet.
  • Meaning fields (OdeNet) and historical example sentences (DTA, ReM) are catalogued but not integrated.
